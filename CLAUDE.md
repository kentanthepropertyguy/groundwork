# Groundwork: project development instructions

Groundwork (tools.kentanthepropertyguy.com) is a static site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript on GitHub Pages, no build step. These are the standing rules for any change, by a person or by Claude. Read them before editing.

## Answer first. Let people explore further.

This is the product principle. It applies to every page, current and future. Keep the interface simple even when the calculation behind it is sophisticated.

1. **The answer comes first.** The first screen, on a phone as well as a desktop, shows what the visitor came for: the development name and its property class, the price or evidence, or the matching developments. Background, method and explanation come after it.
2. **One predictable layout.** Use the Guide Me pages as the reference: one column, a stable search or selection control at the top, results below it, the same heading sizes, widths and spacing as the rest of Groundwork. Do not invent a new page layout, a new dashboard or a second navigation. Pages need not have identical functionality, only the same feel.
3. **Headings are modest.** Page title about 44px on desktop and 32px on a phone, section headings about 32px and 24px. Large display type never pushes the answer below the first phone screen.
4. **Explanations go in disclosures.** Methodology, definitions and long caveats live in a short contextual note or a "How this works" disclosure. Do not delete them, and do not put them in front of the answer.
5. **Limits stay next to the number.** Sample size, price basis (past sales, not a valuation, not a listing) and data-quality limits are shown beside the figure they qualify, in one plain line. Never only in a disclosure, and never repeated in several places.
6. **Fewer cards.** A card needs a reason: it holds one result the visitor will act on. Do not wrap every block in a card.
7. **Explore further, never in the way.** Secondary controls (radius, size, map, compare) sit under the answer and change it in place, without a page reload.
8. **Same rules for every property class.** Condominiums, Executive Condominiums (ECs), cluster housing and mixed developments get the same layout and the same wording discipline. A class is always shown, and prices of different classes are never pooled or compared with each other.
9. **Check it on a 390px phone** (and a desktop) before calling a change finished: what does the visitor see on the first screen?

## Data and meaning (do not change without being asked)

- Development classes come from the shared register (`tools/market-data/register.js`): `condo`, `ec`, `cluster`. Only that file decides admission and class.
- A "typical price" needs at least 5 sales across at least 3 months in a size band (`assets/js/kpt-evidence.js`); weaker evidence is labelled limited, not hidden.
- Past transaction prices are not active listings and not valuations. Say so briefly where prices are shown.
- Distances are straight-line and say so. A place with no map position is counted and named, never silently dropped.
- Analytics are bucketed and anonymous. Typed text, locations, budgets and development names are never sent to analytics or WhatsApp.
- Postal codes have ONE implementation, `assets/js/gw-postal.js` (OneMap lookup, messages, road and development matching); the Map, Budget Search and Property values all call it. Do not write a second lookup. It identifies a property; it never values one, and results from it are labelled past sales, not a valuation.
- The Map's radius is `RADII` in `kpt-map-core.js` (500 m, 1 km, 2 km, 3 km; 1 km default, never widened silently). New launches and GLS sites live in `data/map/upcoming.json` (see `tools/market-data/UPCOMING.md`): no prices or transactions, never a budget match, and an item without a verified position is searchable but not placed on the Map. The radius is offered on EVERY located selection (address, MRT, school, road, development, launch, site) through `placeData`; for a development it is the secondary "Explore nearby" section, with its own link key `nr`, and never changes the price comparison (`r`). The site search (`gw-nav.js`) takes postal codes through the same `gw-postal.js`; its version string inside `gw-nav.js` must be the hash of that file (postal-tests.js checks it). Launches and positions are one repeatable pass: see `tools/market-data/UPCOMING.md` section 0.
- Phone Map sheet (`.bs`): the name, essentials and the radius choices are pinned at the top (`.bs-hd`); the chevron minimises to a slim bar (`S.min`, keeps selection, marker, circle and radius), and × closes and clears. A new selection always opens in full. Research search lists registered launches from `upcoming.json` apart from transaction results (`KPTLG.findLaunches`), never with a price. Tests: `research/tests/mobile-sheet-render.py`, `research/tests/launch-search-tests.js`.
- Map development panel is answer-first: the most meaningful recent price first (`M.lead`: the size with the most recent sales, with its market, size, sale count, period and "past sales, not a valuation"; never a price without enough sales), then the nearest MRT and a school (else a shop or park) in `.around.key`, then sections that open on demand (`details.sx`: prices by size, compare nearby homes, transport schools and amenities, explore nearby developments, data and methodology). Open sections are remembered for the same development (`S.sec`); a development with no typical price opens its sales by size at once. Primary and secondary markets are never combined. A launch or GLS site shows its first three confirmed facts, never a price.
- Layers (`KPT_MAP_VIEW.layers` in `kpt-map-view.js`) is the ONE map-layer control, shared by the Map and Budget Search and remembered for the visit in `sessionStorage` (`kpt.map.layers`): private homes, launches and GLS, MRT on by default; schools and shops and parks off. Add a layer there, never a second control. In Budget Search the matches are the teal dots; every other home or launch is quiet, non-clickable context.
- Budget Search with a place chosen makes the Map the stage: full width, with a compact results panel on the left (desktop) or a bottom sheet that minimises (phone, `S.sheetMin`). With no place the results stay a list. Choosing a card or a dot shows that development on the map; editing keeps the results. The HDB resale layer is the sixth layer there (`hdb`, off by default; see the HDB bullets below).
- HDB research is NOT a second valuation engine. Every HDB price, range, count and fallback comes from `GWV.hdbComps` / `hdbTypes` / `hdbResult` (`assets/js/gw-value.js`) over `data/value/hdb/t/<town>.json`; `assets/js/gw-hdb.js` (`GWH`) only presents it: the research page for one block (`#/hdb/<town>/<block>/<street-slug>[/<flat-type>]`, public facts only, built by `GW_POSTAL.hdbHash`, parsed by `KPTR.parseHash`), the nearby-blocks comparison (each row is that block's own resales of the flat type, at least `HDB_MIN_BLOCK`; block evidence is always labelled apart from street evidence) and the Map panel's short evidence (`GWH.evidenceHtml`). Add a number there only by adding it to gw-value.js first.
- HDB Map layer: block positions are precomputed, never looked up in a visitor's browser. `tools/market-data/fetch-onemap-hdb.js` asks OneMap once per block and keeps only OneMap's own record with the SAME block number and road and a six-digit postal code (`sources/onemap-hdb.json`, with the list of blocks that could not be placed); `build-hdb-map.js` writes `data/map/hdb.json` (positions and resale counts only, no prices). A block with no verified position is left out and is never estimated; its research still works and compares the other blocks on its street. The Map loads `hdb.json` only when the layer is on, an HDB block is linked, or a digit search starts; the layer draws blocks from street level only (clusters with counts below zoom 17), never labels or prices; the panel is block, flat type, recent resales, then "Open full HDB research". Re-run both scripts after any HDB resale refresh (`node research/tests/hdb-tests.js` checks every position against the resale files).
- A postal code is sorted by `GW_POSTAL.classify` (development by name, then HDB block, then landed street, else a place) for the site search, Research's first search, the Map and Property values alike; Research's first search sends HDB to `#/hdb/...`, a development to its Map or Research page, landed to Property prices (the code goes in its box, never the address) and a road or MRT to the Map (`kpt.map.q`, words only). A leading "The" is optional in every name search.
- Reuse before writing: the Map (`assets/js/kpt-map-core.js`, `kpt-map-view.js`) owns place search and map drawing, the Research engines own price evidence, `kpt-find.js` owns the index, inventory freshness and naming. Other pages call them; they do not copy them.
- Budget Search (`tools/what-can-i-buy/`) is the ONE buyer search and results experience. Rules live in `assets/js/kpt-discover.js` (pure, tested by `tools/what-can-i-buy/tests/discover-tests.js`), the page in `kpt-discover-ui.js` and `kpt-discover.css`. The budget is a MAXIMUM; a development is listed with "typical prices within your maximum" (q3 at or under it), "some sales within your maximum" (only q1 at or under it), or, for a new launch with no URA sales, "from Huttons inventory". The headline always describes recent sales, never current availability. Up to 5% over is a separate group, off by default. Bedrooms are NEVER inferred: they apply to new launches only, from Huttons inventory checked within 7 days; resale is never filtered by bedrooms, and the control says so. `/buy/` only redirects here (keeping `?resume=1`, the HDB planner handoff and the old Buy answers in this tab). Guide Me, the HDB planner and the menu all link to this one address. `kpt-buyer.js` and `kpt-engine.js`-based Buy logic is kept but no page uses it. A budget handed over from a planner (`from:'hdb-upgrade'`, range `low`–`high`) opens the prefilled form first (top of the range as the maximum, the source said once) and shows results only after Show developments; never straight to islandwide results, and no second questionnaire.
- Budget Search history: one page, two history entries (`history.state = {kd:'f'|'r', snap}`). `pushState` only when Show developments is pressed; layers, radius, selection and the Edit panel never add entries; `popstate` switches between the prefilled form and the results. A planner handoff or saved search opens the prefilled form first.
- Research is ONE page with three options, one open at a time (`research/index.html` + `KPTLG.landingHtml`): a development (search, Ken's projects, A–Z), Property prices & past sales (Property Values mounted in the page: `GWV.pageHtml` then `GWV.mount(host)`), and What can this budget buy? (the Budget Search form in embed mode: `KPD_CFG.embed`). Addresses: `#/` , `#/prices`, `#/budget`, `#/browse` (development with A–Z open); `/projects/` redirects to `#/browse`. Switching options uses `history.replaceState` (no history entries) and keeps entries because the home element is built once and put back after an analysis. Prices and Budget load their scripts the first time they open, from `<template id="gwLazy-…">` blocks (so file hashes stay checked). The Budget results stay on `tools/what-can-i-buy/` (Show developments saves the search on the device and sets `kpt.find.from='research'`, so Back returns to Research). Typed entries (search text, budget, postal code) never go in an address, analytics or WhatsApp; the Budget data version strings in Research must equal the Budget page's (`research/tests/unified-tests.js` checks).
- Development search → Map: a positioned development (in `places.json` or `upcoming.json`) opens `research/map/#p=<id>`; others open `#/p/<id>`. The Map's "Open full analysis" stores `kpt.map.out` ({id, hash, history length}); the analysis then shows "← Back to Map" (`history.back()` when nothing was visited in between, otherwise the stored Map address). Launches without sales show confirmed facts and location only.
- The dedicated Map page (`research/map/`) is full-screen under the header (`body.kpm` flex column, no footer, no page scroll). Data and sources, the licence line, credentials and privacy are one panel (`#sources`) opened from "Data & sources" in the attribution strip (or `[data-sources]` links / `#sources`); OneMap and SLA attribution stays visible. Panels scroll on their own (`overscroll-behavior:contain`).
- Map first view: `KPT_MAP_VIEW` fits the bulk of private-home positions to the usable map area (fractional zoom via a temporary `zoomSnap`); deep links (development, MRT, road, postal, area) frame the place and its ring. The Research Map remembers its position in `sessionStorage` (`kpt.map.pos`) and restores it only on Back/Forward or reload, never on a fresh link.
- Page heads share one scale in `gw.css` (44px desktop, 32px phone, left-aligned), one 12px field style and one compact `.gw-ken` band; `research/tests/consistency-render.py` checks it.

## Working rules

- Every script and stylesheet reference carries `?v=<10 hex characters of the file's sha256>`; change a file and its references change with it. The Map files use one shared date version instead, and the budget tool must request the same one (a test checks).
- Data is rebuilt only with `tools/market-data/refresh-all.js` (see `tools/market-data/REFRESH.md`); do not hand-edit generated files.
- Run the test suites for what you touched, compare with the previous results, and fix a failure rather than editing its test. Browser tests stub the OneMap tiles, so look at the real Map once after a release.
- Keep releases small and independently testable. Do not bundle unrelated work (for example calculator retirement or Exit Analysis) into a UX or data change.
- Planner (`tools/planner/`, `assets/js/gw-answer.js` engine, `gw-answer-ui.js` page): ONE answer, "Your maximum property budget", the highest price both the buyer's funds and income allow (`assess().budget`, rounded DOWN to the nearest $1,000 so the figure shown always passes; `exactBudget()` is the exact whole-dollar price, shown in the breakdown only). There is no default reserve or cushion; `cushion.amount` is 0 unless the buyer sets one under advanced options. `assess().fundsPrice()` is the price funds alone support (income cap off), `borrow.max` is the income-supported loan, `evaluate().loan` the loan actually needed at a price, and `limitedBy` ('funds' | 'income' | 'choice' | 'payments') drives the single sentence under the figures. The buyer's own price shows required loan, monthly mortgage and cash/CPF; a shortfall appears only when it is above $0. Legal and valuation fees, renovation and furnishing are NOT in the maximum; the breakdown says so and "Set aside" (More options) is the buyer's own way to hold money back. The HDB upgrade planner (`tools/hdb-upgrade/assumptions.js`) also defaults its reserve to $0; its estimated $5,000 purchase costs remain. Tests: `tools/financial-tests/afford-tests.js` (independent solver, single and two-buyer cases), `golden-tests.js`, `afford-render.py`. Regenerate `golden.json` only for a deliberate engine change and say which scenarios moved.
- The buyer's view of an answer (`assets/js/gw-buyer-view.js`, `GW_VIEW.build(result, {otpEntered})`) is the ONE place that decides what a buyer reads from a Planner result; it does no arithmetic and changes no rule. With no price the headline is the maximum, with a price it is that price's verdict ("$X short", "Within your budget", or "Projected shortfall"), always followed by one sentence (the limit, or the cash and CPF needed with the minimum cash), exactly TWO supporting figures (monthly mortgage and borrowing capacity; or bank loan and monthly mortgage), one dates line and one eligibility line. Everything else (payment dates, assumptions including the loan tenure, the working) is a closed disclosure. Three questions are never merged: estimated affordability (the headline), eligibility (always "not confirmed"; never say verified, approved or qualified) and payment dates (basis: Not checked | Standard schedule | Your option date | Your monthly amounts; outcome: Covered | First short on <date> | Projected shortfall). A shortfall that depends on a later payment is labelled "Projected" and is never shown as a plain "$X short". With a home sold first, what is in hand now is stated apart from what the sale adds "only once it completes", and the first payments (option fee, exercise, stamp duty) above what is in hand now are flagged as a projection. There is no price slider: the price is a plain optional field, and a price handed from Research ("Can I afford this?", `gw.fin.v1.buyPrice` + `buyFrom` = typical | asking, tab-only, never in an address) is named until the buyer changes it. Add wording to the view first, never in the page; tests: `tools/financial-tests/buyer-view-tests.js` (independent figures) and `afford-render.py`.
- Connected journeys (Package 2): the site header, menu and footer are Buy · Sell · Research · Tools (the Map lives under Research), generated from the one list in `gw-nav.js` by `apply-p15-nav.py`-style regeneration; never hand-edit one page's header. Shared figures (`gw.fin.v1`, keyed by `data-share`) are tab-only: a field that carries a share key is filled from it once on load, and a figure the buyer has typed on that page wins over a shared one. Never share `debts` (the Planner asks for loan instalments separately, so sharing would double count) and never carry a calculator's `0` ownership default. Sale proceeds hands its sale to the Planner through `gw.sell.v1` (tab only, one use, used only when the Planner is opened from "What can I buy next?" at `tools/planner/#sell`; cleared by "Clear all my figures"). The Planner's cash and CPF always mean what the buyer has NOW; the sale is counted on its own, only once it completes, and only when the Planner would count it anyway. Tests: `nav-tests.js`, `connect-render.py`.
- Planner → Budget Search handoff: `kpt.handoff` `{from:'planner', budget:{low, high, single}}` (tab-only; never in a URL, analytics or WhatsApp). "Explore properties within my budget" opens Budget Search prefilled ("From your plan"); location, sale type, property type and bedrooms stay optional and editable, and a saved search (`find2`) is shared by Guide Me, Budget Search and Research.
