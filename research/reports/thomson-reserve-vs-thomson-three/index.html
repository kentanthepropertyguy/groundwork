Thomson Reserve or Thomson Three?

A new launch and its resale neighbour on Bright Hill Drive, compared on lease, layouts, actual resale prices, payment timing and trade-offs, so you can judge a Thomson Reserve price when you see one.

Updated 10 Oct 2026URA data to Aug 2026Thomson Reserve prices: not released

Disclosure

Ken Tan (Huttons Asia, CEA R007903D) is involved in marketing Thomson Reserve. This report is written to be balanced: it sets out where Thomson Three may be the better choice as well as Thomson Reserve, and every figure comes from the sources listed at the end.

The short answer

Two neighbours on Bright Hill Drive, priced on different terms.

AI-assisted interpretation

Thomson Reserve is a new launch of 1,268 homes, due to complete in 2031 with a fresh 99-year lease3. Thomson Three, on the same road, is a completed development of 445 homes whose lease started in 20124,8. One is bought off the plan at a price the developer sets; the other is bought from an owner at a price the market has been setting, sale by sale, for years.

  1. What premium am I paying over a comparable resale home?Not known until prices are released. Thomson Three’s 732 sq ft 2-bedrooms resold at a median of $2,080 psf over the last 24 months and its 3-bedrooms at $2,223 psf4 Calculated. “Try a price” works out the premium per sq ft and in total the moment you have a price.
  2. What am I getting in return?A new building, about 13.6 more years of lease, more than 80 facilities and a sheltered link to the MRT3 Developer information. In exchange: no home to live in or let until 2031, and no resale record yet.
  3. Will the price still make sense when I sell?That depends mostly on the entry price, and nobody can know in advance. In the developer-sale comparison later in this report (19 developments chosen by a fixed rule), median resale prices were higher than median developer-sale prices in most cases, while the premium over older resale homes nearby narrowed in 22 of 254 Calculated. Those are groups of sales, not individual buyers’ returns, and not a forecast for Thomson Reserve. See Is the new-launch premium worth paying?

The main differences

  • Lease. About 98.7 years left at Thomson Reserve against 85.1 at Thomson Three today, a gap of about 13.6 years that stays the same over time Calculated.
  • Timing. Thomson Three can usually be lived in or let within a few months of buying (subject to any existing tenancy). Thomson Reserve’s expected vacant possession is 28 Feb 2031, about 52 months from now3 Calculated.
  • Scale and facilities. 1,268 homes and more than 80 facilities in three clubs, against 445 homes and a smaller set of facilities3,6.
  • Layouts. Thomson Reserve is mostly compact 2-bedrooms: 56% of its homes are 592–775 sq ft3 Calculated. Thomson Three’s 2-bedroom is 732 sq ft and its 3-bedrooms are 1,033–1,141 sq ft4.
  • Price certainty. Thomson Three’s prices are a record of what buyers have paid. Thomson Reserve’s will be the developer’s asking prices, before any resale record exists.

What remains uncertain

  • Thomson Reserve’s prices, until the developer releases them. Nothing on this page is an estimate of them.
  • Final sizes and details: the developer states areas are approximate and subject to change3.
  • Thomson Three’s condition, upkeep and MCST finances, which are not assessed here.
  • How 1,268 new homes completing in 2031 will affect resale and rental demand around Bright Hill, for both developments.

Location

Both sit on Bright Hill Drive in District 20, near Upper Thomson MRT on the Thomson–East Coast Line, which opened in 20215. Thomson Reserve’s main entrance has a sheltered link towards the station; the developer describes “direct connectivity”1,3 Developer information. Portal figures put Thomson Three about 410–440 m from the station6,7,8 Reported. Both are near Ai Tong School2,8,9, and Thomson Plaza is across the road from Thomson Three9. Distance alone says nothing about Primary 1 admission chances, which depend on the school’s rules each year.

Facilities and practical differences

  • Facilities. Thomson Reserve: more than 80, in three clubs, including two 50 m lap pools3 Developer information. Thomson Three: a lap pool, children’s pool, gym, sauna, function room, karaoke, BBQ pits and playground, with no tennis court6,9 Reported.
  • Construction next door. Thomson Reserve is being built until about 2031. A Thomson Three buyer moving in now will live beside a construction site for several years; how much that matters depends on the stack. Ask which Thomson Three stacks face Bright Hill Drive and the site.
  • Parking. Thomson Reserve has 1,021 lots for 1,268 homes, about 0.8 per home3 Calculated. Thomson Three’s count is not in the sources reviewed.
  • Views. The developer says over 45% of Thomson Reserve’s stacks face the Central Catchment Nature Reserve3 Developer information. Views at either development depend on the stack and floor.

Is the new-launch premium worth paying?

A fair premium for the home is not the same as a premium you get back.

Sourced facts and calculations with AI-assisted interpretation, marked

A new launch usually costs more than older resale homes nearby. That alone doesn’t make it overpriced. But a premium that is fair for a better home can still lead to a weak return if the entry price is high, and a new launch bought at a premium can still rise further. This section looks at both, with the same tests for both developments.

Across these 19 developments, median resale prices in the 24 months to Aug 2026 were 8% above the median developer-sale prices recorded in 2021–22, for the same size band. 6 of 29 were lower. Over the same period, older resale homes in the same market segments rose more in 23 of 29 cases, and the median premium over older resale nearby narrowed from 31% to 23% Calculated.

What this suggests: in this period, the premium at which these developers sold was generally not fully kept in later resale prices, and much of the price rise came from the market as a whole rather than from the developments themselves. Results varied widely, so the entry price and the specific development mattered AI-assisted interpretation.

  • These are groups of developer sales and later resales, not matched transactions: a change in median prices does not establish any individual buyer’s profit or loss.
  • A narrowing premium does not mean the original premium was unjustified. Buyers may have paid it for a new home, a longer lease or the facilities, and received that value in use.
  • A development can rise in price while its premium over older homes narrows: most of these did both (median 7.7% higher, with the premium narrower in 22 of 25 cases).
  • These outcomes come from one period and other locations. They do not predict how Thomson Reserve will perform.

Limits: these are not launch-day prices and not investors’ returns. URA records don’t identify units, so no resale is matched to its original purchase; the homes sold in each period differ in floor, facing, stack and exact size, and the sales were made at different dates in different market conditions. The 29 results are not independent: 19 developments contribute up to two size bands each, and all share one market period (sales in 2021–22, followed by rising prices for older resale homes). None is in District 20. Round-trip costs of about 5.7% of the price (Buyer’s Stamp Duty, legal fees and a 2% selling commission plus GST) would also come out of any gain Calculated.

D. When Thomson Reserve becomes a resale home

  • From completion, buyers will compare its homes with other resale homes, including Thomson Three and any newer developments nearby. The launch premium is not automatically kept.
  • Up to 1,268 homes will complete at about the same time. Owners who sell or let soon after will compete with each other, and with Thomson Three.
  • What happens then depends on the entry price, the market, supply nearby, the project’s quality and buyer demand. This report makes no forecast.
  • The same applies to Thomson Three in reverse: a large new development next door adds competing resale and rental supply from 2031.

E. At what price does Thomson Reserve become worth considering?

There is no single answer, and this report doesn’t set a price threshold. When prices are released, each layout can be checked against the same evidence:

  • Same-size resale. The premium per sq ft and in total over Thomson Three, and whether a same-budget Thomson Three home gives more space.
  • Newest resale nearby. Whether the price is above or below Jadescape (lease from 2018), the newest resale development in the district.
  • Developer-sale premiums, as a reference point. How the premium compares with those in the comparison above (median 31% over older resale nearby, highest 49%). A reference, not a threshold: it says nothing on its own about whether a price is good value.
  • Layout and total price. What you pay for usable space, and whether the total fits your loan and cash.
  • Stack, floor, facing and view. A better stack is worth more only if you value it; compare like with like.
  • Lease, timing and project advantages. About 13.6 more years of lease, a new building and its facilities, but no use until completion.
  • Competing supply. Other new launches and newer resale homes buyers could choose instead, now and at completion.
  • Your objective and margin of safety. Own stay, investment or upgrading; for an investment, how far prices could fall before selling would lose money after costs.
How a price will be assessed, once official prices are out. For each layout, and where it matters for individual units, using all of the checks above together. No single figure decides it, and there is no score.
Attractive: compares well with comparable resale homes on both price per sq ft and total price, after allowing for size, layout, floor, facing and lease, with room to spare for the buyer’s objective.
Reasonable: the premium over comparable resale homes is explained by tangible advantages such as the lease, a newer building and the layout, and is in line with the reference evidence.
Selective: justified only for particular units, stacks or buyer objectives, not across the layout.
Difficult to justify: the premium is hard to explain by tangible advantages, or comparable resale homes clearly offer more for the same total budget.
Insufficient evidence to assess: when there are too few comparable resales of a similar size to judge.
Assessments are made only against the developer’s official prices, are reviewed by Ken before they appear, and are not investment recommendations.

Financial considerations

New launch or resale: when the money goes out.

Sourced facts and calculations

Thomson Reserve: new launch

  1. 5% on bookingCash, when you take the option to purchase.
  2. 15% within 8 weeks of the option dateYou sign the sale and purchase agreement in this period. Cash or CPF. Stamp duty is due within 14 days of signing it.
  3. 40% during constructionIn 6 stages (10%, 10%, 5%, 5%, 5%, 5%), as each is certified. You pay from cash or CPF until your down payment is used up; only then does the bank loan pay. With a 75% loan, the loan starts paying part of the foundation stage; with a 55% loan, you pay 45% of the price yourself, up to the partition-walls stage, and the loan starts at roofing.
  4. 25% at TOPWhen the building gets its Temporary Occupation Permit; expected vacant possession 28 Feb 20313.
  5. 15% at completion (CSC)The Certificate of Statutory Completion, usually some time after TOP.

Standard progressive payment schedule17. The booking fee is cash. For a first housing loan at 75%, at least 5% of the price must be cash; at 55%, at least 10%15. Instalments start once the loan is drawn and grow stage by stage. Actual funding depends on your loan approval, how much CPF you may use and your payment arrangements.

Thomson Three: resale

  1. Option feeUsually 1% in cash, for the seller’s option to purchase.
  2. Exercise the optionUsually a further 4% within about 2–3 weeks, bringing the down payment to 5%. Stamp duty is due within 14 days.
  3. Completion, about 8–12 weeks laterThe rest of the down payment from cash or CPF (at least 5% of the price in cash in total for a first loan at 75%), then the whole bank loan is drawn at once.
  4. Full instalments from the first monthOn the whole loan. You can move in or let the home out.

Usual market practice, not a statutory schedule; the option sets the actual terms. Loan limits15.

Stamp duty

Buyer’s Stamp Duty and ABSD apply in the same way to both, on the purchase price10,11. At Thomson Three’s median 2-bedroom resale price of $1,523,000, BSD is $45,750 Calculated. The difference is timing for second-home buyers, below.

If you own an HDB flat or another home

  • Buying a second home means paying ABSD up front: 20% for a Singapore Citizen buying a second residential property11.
  • A married couple can have it refunded only if every condition is met. They buy the new home in their two names only, and at least one of them is a Singapore Citizen. When they buy, each owns no more than one other residential property; if each owns a different one, both must be sold. Every such property must be sold within 6 months, neither spouse may buy another residential property in the meantime, and the couple must still be married at the sale. If one spouse owns a home and the other doesn’t, only that home has to be sold13,14.
  • The 6 months run from the purchase date for a completed home such as Thomson Three, and from the TOP or CSC date, whichever is earlier, for a home under construction such as Thomson Reserve. The refund is claimed within 6 months of the sale14.
  • So a qualifying couple who buy Thomson Reserve could stay in their current home until the new one is completed. At Thomson Three they would have to sell within 6 months of buying, or sell first. IRAS decides each case.

Financing

  • The same loan rules apply to both. For a first housing loan, banks can lend up to 75% of the price when the tenure is 30 years or less and the loan ends by age 65. A longer tenure (up to 35 years for a private home) or a loan running past 65 is allowed, with a lower limit of 55%15.
  • Joint borrowers: the age used is the income-weighted average age, with income counted as banks count it (after a 30% haircut on variable and rental income)15. Groundwork’s TDSR calculator works it out.
  • Total Debt Servicing Ratio: all monthly debt repayments, including the new loan, within 55% of income. Banks test the loan at 4% or your rate after any lock-in period, if higher16.
  • At Thomson Three, the whole loan is drawn on completion and instalments start in full. At Thomson Reserve, the loan pays stages only after your down payment has been paid, so instalments start later and grow until completion17.
  • A loan application is assessed on your income and debts when you apply, for the full loan, whichever you buy.

Assumptions and limits

  • Rules as checked by Groundwork on 9 Oct 2026. They can change; your bank, CPF Board and IRAS decide.
  • Actual funding depends on your bank’s loan approval, how much CPF you are allowed to use, and the payment arrangements in your sale agreement. The order shown (cash first, then cash or CPF, then the loan) is the standard sequence, not a guarantee of how your purchase will be funded.
  • The progressive schedule is the standard one. Thomson Reserve’s sale and purchase agreement sets the actual terms, and the timing of each stage depends on construction.
  • Resale payment timing follows usual market practice, not a statutory schedule.

Buyer suitability

Own stay, investment and upgrading are different decisions.

AI-assisted interpretation

For own stay

Paying more can be worthwhile for a home you will live in: a newer building, a longer lease, the facilities and convenience, even if the price does not rise much. Thomson Three can be lived in now; Thomson Reserve from about 2031. Compare the same size and the same budget, not headline psf.

For investment

The entry price and the market you will sell into matter most. Weigh the opportunity cost of the premium, round-trip costs of about 5.7%, interest, the holding period (Seller’s Stamp Duty applies to a sale within 4 years of buying, for homes bought from 4 Jul 202512), rent (now at Thomson Three, from 2031 at Thomson Reserve) and competing supply. A lower psf does not make Thomson Three the better investment, and a longer lease does not make Thomson Reserve the better one.

For upgraders

Timing can matter as much as price: progressive payments, whether you qualify for the ABSD refund route, when you sell, how CPF is used and the cash needed in between. Check your MOP, loan and cash flow before choosing.

Buyers who want a newer property

Thomson Reserve is the clear match on age and lease. The question is the size of the premium: use the reference points and Try a price once prices are out.

Buyers who want a lower entry price

Thomson Three’s 1-bedrooms resold for about $900k and its 2-bedrooms for about $1.52m. Thomson Reserve’s 592 sq ft 2-bedroom would cost less than Thomson Three’s typical 2-bedroom only if priced below $2,573 psf Calculated.

Final assessment and next steps

A balanced view, and what to check.

AI-assisted interpretation

On today’s evidence, the choice turns on three things: whether you can wait until 2031, how much a newer building and longer lease are worth to you, and the price Thomson Reserve is released at. For own stay, paying a premium for a better home can be reasonable. For investment, the evidence argues for care with large premiums: in the comparison above, they were generally not fully kept in later resale prices, even where prices rose. Thomson Three gives price certainty, immediate use and a sales record you can check, in an older building with a shorter lease.
